Tan safely

The honest page about ultraviolet

Most salons bury this. We would rather you read it, because a customer who understands their skin comes back for years and a customer who burns does not come back at all.

Start here

What a sunbed is actually doing

Ultraviolet light triggers melanin production in your skin. That is the tan. It is also, unavoidably, a stress response — your skin is darkening because it has been damaged, and that damage accumulates over a lifetime.

None of which means you should not use one. It means the dose matters enormously, and that the difference between a tan you enjoy and skin you regret is almost entirely about how quickly you build up.

Two things worth knowing

  • A base tan is not sun cream. It is worth roughly SPF 3. Pack the factor 30 regardless.
  • Burning once is worse than tanning often. Sunburn, especially before the age of 25, is the strongest single predictor of melanoma risk. Short sessions, spaced out, never burning — that is the whole strategy.

You should not use our sunbeds if you

  • Are under 18 — this is a legal restriction, not a house rule
  • Have skin type I: always burns, never tans
  • Have a large number of moles or freckles
  • Have had skin cancer, or a family history of melanoma
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Take photosensitising medication — some antibiotics, acne treatments, antidepressants and St John's wort all qualify
  • Have a skin condition made worse by UV, or recent laser or chemical peel treatment
  • Are sunburnt right now, from any source

If any of these apply, a spray tan gives you the colour with none of the ultraviolet. Ask us about it.

The law

What UK sunbed regulation actually requires

18

Strictly over-18s

The Sunbeds (Regulation) Act 2010 makes it an offence for a business in England and Wales to let anyone under 18 use a commercial sunbed — or to be present in the restricted zone around one. Operators face fines of up to £20,000. We ask for photo ID from every new customer.

0.3

The irradiance limit

Under the harmonised standard EN 60335-2-27, commercial sunbeds must not exceed a total erythema-weighted UV irradiance of 0.3 W/m² — the strictest limit internationally. Our beds are set and serviced to stay within it, and we log every lamp change.

3

What a salon owes you

Protective eyewear available, clear information about risk, a supervised session time set to your skin type, and staff on site who can answer a question rather than point at a laminated sign. That is the floor, not the ceiling.

House rules

The six we never bend

Photo ID, every new customer

Driving licence, passport or PASS card. Bring it on your first visit and we will not need to ask again.

Goggles on, all the way through

Eyelids do not stop UV. Long-term exposure is linked to cataracts and ocular melanoma. Free pair on your first visit.

24 hours between sessions

Your tan is still developing after you leave. Two sessions in a day gives you a burn, not a better colour.

A skin assessment first

Two minutes of questions before your first session, and we revisit it if you tell us anything has changed.

Timers set by us

Nobody sets their own time up. Every session is dialled in at the desk against your recorded skin type.

We will say no

If you are burnt, on the wrong medication, or asking for a jump we do not think your skin will take, we will offer you a spray tan instead.

Where to read more. The NHS, Cancer Research UK and the British Association of Dermatologists all publish plain guidance on UV exposure and skin cancer risk. If anything on this page raises a question about a mole or a change in your skin, speak to your GP rather than to us — we sell tanning, they diagnose skin.
